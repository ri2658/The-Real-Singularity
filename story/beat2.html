Beat 2

Balls in bowls, balls on hilltops

A mixture of two liquids, like oil and vinegar, slowly separates into patches. Some arrangements of those patches are stable, like a ball resting in a bowl. Others are unstable, like a ball balanced on a hilltop: perfectly still, until the smallest nudge sends it rolling.

Try it yourself

Three demos, in story order

The step-by-step simulator

A traditional simulation always rolls downhill, so it can only ever settle into a stable state. Try the S-shape with a tiny nudge.

Falling off a hilltop

Balance the mixture on a hilltop, nudge it, and time the fall. A 10× smaller nudge only buys about 1.5 more time units.

Fixing the PINN's guesses

We hand the PINN's rough answers to the simulator: the stable one settles into place, the unstable one falls off.

So far

The simulator matches all five known resting states, and how strongly each one pulls nearby shapes in or pushes them away. But because it always rolls downhill, it can never stop on a hilltop. A PINN can aim at those hilltop states directly. Each tool wins a different problem.
