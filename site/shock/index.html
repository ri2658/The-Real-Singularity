How a smooth wave turns into a cliff

We start with a smooth wave of moving fluid. Fluid on the left side moves right, fluid on the right side moves left, so everything heads toward the middle. Each blue line in the top picture follows one bit of fluid as it moves at its starting speed.

The faster bits catch up with the slower ones ahead of them. At time t ≈ 0.318 the lines first cross at the middle (x = 0), and the smooth wave becomes a sudden jump, called a shock. Press Play to watch it happen. Sharp jumps like this are exactly what trips up the PINN on the next pages.

The bottom picture shows the fluid's speed at each position. The thickness menu sets how thick (viscous) the fluid is: thick fluid rounds the jump off a little, thin fluid makes it almost a vertical wall.
