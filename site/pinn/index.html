Our PINN, running live in your browser

1The question

A PINN is a neural network that learns to obey the equation directly, instead of copying answers someone already computed. This one is small (8 layers of 20 neurons) and was trained on the thick fluid, the easy case where the jump in the middle stays gentle. Once trained, it answers instantly: pick a time and it draws the fluid's speed at every position.

2Try it

Drag time from 0 to 1 and watch the middle of the wave steepen. Drag position to read the PINN's speed at one point; every new picture is computed on the spot.

Loading the model.

3Why it is instant

Training is the slow part: this network took about 20 minutes to train. After that it is just a formula with 3,021 numbers in it. Plugging in a position and a time takes a few thousand multiplications, so the browser draws a whole curve in a blink, with no grid and no time stepping.

4Key takeaways

  • Once trained, a PINN answers instantly, at any position and time you ask for.
  • This model was trained on the thick fluid, the easy case, where it lands within about 0.05% of the exact answer.
  • The catch is the training, and what happens when the jump gets sharp: that is the next page.
