Correcting a PINN

A physics-informed neural network gave us rough guesses for two steady states of the Allen–Cahn equation: the stable hump and the unstable S. Both guesses have a small training loss. Here a plain forward-Euler solver takes each guess as its starting curve. One heals into the exact answer; the other heals for a while, then falls off.

Why the hump heals. The hump is a bowl: every small change to it shrinks, the slowest at rate 8.69 per time unit, so the PINN's error drops about 6,000× per time unit. The solver turns a rough guess into the exact answer, which is what classical methods are good at.

Why the S falls off. The S is a hilltop: it has exactly one way to change that grows, at rate 1.53. The solver first removes the PINN's error in every other direction, so the curve moves toward S. The tiny leftover piece in the unstable direction keeps growing, and the curve rolls into a hump. The PINN's loss can't tell a bowl from a hilltop: both are exact solutions with zero residual.
